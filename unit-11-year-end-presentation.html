Grow and Glow

My Dream Home

Four slides. Two minutes. Your own design, your own voice.

Press → or Space to begin. Theme changes the colours.

Warm-Up

First Impressions

Start with a place you can already picture.

If you could wake up in any place tomorrow, where would it be?

Go further: What is the first thing you would see from the window?

Possible answer
A small house near the sea. I would see the water before I saw the garden.

Describe your ideal room in three adjectives.

Go further: Which adjective would a friend disagree with?

Possible answer
Bright, tidy, and calm. My friend would say calm is impossible with my desk.
Grammar in Context

Four Slides, Four Patterns

Present and future for the place. Adjectives and passive for the house. Modals for what you can do. Present perfect for who lives there.

Slide one: My dream home is in the mountains. I chose it because I love snow. Add one future sentence.

Go further: What future form sounds most natural here?

Possible answer
I will spend every winter there. 'Will' sounds like a real plan, while 'going to' sounds like a guess.

Slide two: The walls are painted blue. Why is passive useful?

Go further: What would the sentence be in active voice?

Possible answer
Someone painted the walls blue. Active sounds odd because nobody cares who did it.
Slide One

Where Is It?

Country, city, or nature. Give a reason a listener can feel.

Where exactly is your dream home, and why there?

Go further: What would you miss if you lived there?

Possible answer
It is in the mountains of Hokkaido. I would miss the convenience of a shop on every corner.

What is the journey from your real home to your dream home like?

Go further: Which part of the journey do you enjoy?

Possible answer
A train, then a bus, then a short walk. I enjoy the walk because the air changes.

Slide Two

Slides Two & Four

What it looks like, and who lives there. Choose three details for each.

Describe the outside of your dream home.

Go further: What would a neighbour notice first?

Possible answer
It is a small wooden house with a blue door and a wide garden. A neighbour would notice the garden first.

Describe the room you would spend most time in.

Go further: What object would sit on the desk?

Possible answer
The kitchen, because it is bright and spacious. On the desk there would be a small plant.

Who lives with you in your dream home?

Go further: How long have you known them?

Possible answer
My family and one older dog. I have known my best friend since first year, and she lives nearby.

What has changed in your life because of the people you live with?

Go further: What have they taught you?

Possible answer
I have become more patient. My sister taught me to wait before I answer.
Slide Three

What Can You Do There?

Use modals. Talk about activities inside and outside.

What can you do in your dream home that you cannot do now?

Go further: Who would you invite there first?

Possible answer
I can swim in the garden pool whenever I want. I would invite my whole club.

What could go wrong in this perfect place?

Go further: How would you fix it?

Possible answer
The garden would need too much work. I would ask my family to help and keep one small flower bed only.

Challenge

Two Minutes

Four slides, one minute each at most. Do not read your notes.

Which slide will take you the longest?

Go further: What will you cut if you run over time?

Possible answer
Slide two, because I love describing rooms. I will cut the furniture list.

How will you end the talk?

Go further: What question will you leave the class with?

Possible answer
I will ask where they would build their dream home if money did not matter.
Compliment Circle

Listen and Respond

After each talk, give one specific compliment. No vague praise.

What makes a compliment feel real rather than polite?

Go further: Give one you received this year.

Possible answer
Naming the exact thing. My teacher said my story had a good last line, and I remembered it.

What would you say if a classmate forgot half their talk?

Go further: Which words help them try again?

Possible answer
I would say the picture part was clear. Then I would ask about the part they skipped.

What did you learn about a classmate from their dream home?

Go further: What surprised you?

Possible answer
That my quiet friend wants a huge kitchen. I had no idea she loved cooking.
Wrap-Up

One Year of Speaking

You described a picture, reported a message, explained a process, and gave a talk. Keep going.

Which deck from this year changed how you speak?

Go further: What will you take into next year?

Possible answer
The story deck. I still look for small details before I describe anything.
