Warm-Up

Start With the End

Describe the finished thing first. Then walk backwards to the beginning.

What is the last step in making your favourite snack at home?

Go further: What has to happen before that step?

Possible answer
You eat it, but before that someone puts it on a plate. The plate part matters more than people think.

Which process at school do you know well enough to teach?

Go further: Where would a new student get stuck?

Possible answer
Printing in the library. They would get stuck at choosing the right tray.
Grammar in Context

Sequence and Passive

First, then, after that, finally. The beans are sorted. The water is added.

Chocolate: the beans are roasted, then they are ground, and finally the mixture is shaped. Why use passive here?

Go further: What changes if you name the worker in every step?

Possible answer
The step matters more than the person. Naming the worker would slow the explanation down.

Describe making tea using four sequence words and one passive sentence.

Go further: Which stage needs the most detail?

Possible answer
First the water is boiled. Then the cup is warmed. After that the leaves are added, and finally we wait. The waiting needs detail.
Process Prompts

Explain It Clearly

A good explanation leaves out nothing important and nothing pointless.

Explain how to make a sandwich to someone who has never used a kitchen.

Go further: Which step would they get wrong without your warning?

Possible answer
You put the bread down first. They might forget the knife is sharp, so I would warn them.

How is a train ticket checked at your station? Walk through it.

Go further: What happens if the machine rejects the ticket?

Possible answer
You put the ticket in the gate and it opens. If it fails, you show it to the staff at the side.

Describe how a photo goes from your phone to a friend's screen.

Go further: Which part of that journey is invisible to you?

Possible answer
I send it and they open it. The signal travelling between is invisible to me.

Speaking Practice

Teach Your Process

Choose a recipe, a craft, or a routine. Teach it in one minute.

Which process will you teach, and who would actually need it?

Go further: What question will your listener ask at the end?

Possible answer
I will teach how to fold a paper star. My little cousin would need it, and she will ask how to make the corners neat.

Where does your explanation usually become unclear?

Go further: What would fix that part?

Possible answer
I rush the middle. I should stop and say the same step twice, in different words.
Picture Sequence

Four Frames

Four pictures tell a whole process. Add the missing frames in words.

Frame one shows raw ingredients on a table. What happens in frame two?

Go further: What changed between them?

Possible answer
Someone washes the vegetables. The change is that the dirt is gone and the water is running.

The last frame shows a finished cake. What went wrong in frame three?

Go further: How did the cook fix it?

Possible answer
The cake sank in the middle. They covered it with fruit and nobody noticed.

Challenge

Explain Without Hands

Describe a process with your hands still. Your partner must draw it from your words.

Which process would be hardest to explain without gestures?

Go further: Which words replace the gesture?

Possible answer
Folding paper. I would need words like corner, edge, and towards me.

When your partner's drawing is wrong, how do you correct it kindly?

Go further: What do you say instead of that is wrong?

Possible answer
I say I can see a different shape here. Then I repeat the step more slowly.
Go Deeper

Why Steps Matter

A process is a way of thinking in order.

Why do people skip steps when they explain something they know well?

Go further: How do you catch yourself doing it?

Possible answer
They forget what it felt like not to know. I watch the listener's face and stop when they look lost.

Which process in your life have you changed to make it better?

Go further: What did you remove?

Possible answer
My homework routine. I removed my phone from the desk and the work takes half the time.

When is it better to show a process than to describe it?

Go further: Can you think of one from this week?

Possible answer
When the shapes matter. I could not explain the new dance step until someone showed it once.
Wrap-Up

Explain It Once More

Pick one process. Say it in four sentences: first, then, after that, finally.

Which process will you explain at home tonight?

Go further: Who will listen?

Possible answer
How to make instant noodles. My brother will listen, probably while complaining.
