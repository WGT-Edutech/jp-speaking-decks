Warm-Up

Look Closer

Forget the whole scene for a moment. Find one small thing most people would miss.

On your way to school, what small detail would you draw if you had to paint the journey?

Go further: What would that detail tell a stranger about your morning?

Possible answer
The crack in the pavement outside the konbini. I pass it every day and never look at it, but it tells you the rain comes in hard here.

Think of a place you know well. Which sound reaches you first when you close your eyes and picture it?

Go further: What changes about the place when that sound disappears?

Possible answer
The crossing bell near my station. Without it the whole street feels like a silent film, even though the shops are still busy.
Grammar in Context

First, Then, After That, Finally

Sequence words carry a listener through a story. Adjectives give the listener something to see.

My morning was a shabby mess. First I missed the bus. Then my umbrella broke. After that I found my homework still on the kitchen table. Finally I laughed. Which moment would you keep if you had to cut two?

Go further: What would you add in its place to make the story clearer?

Possible answer
I would keep the broken umbrella. The other moments are things that happened, but the umbrella is something I could see and feel.

Describe your worst morning using four sequence words and two adjectives. Keep it under forty words.

Go further: Which adjective did you choose carefully, and why that one?

Possible answer
First the alarm failed. Then the train was crowded and hot. After that my friend called me late. Finally I walked into class with wet shoes.
Picture Prompts

Three Pictures, One Story

A strong story picks one odd detail and follows it.

A stranger leaves a single yellow umbrella on a train seat. What do you think happened before the train arrived?

Go further: What does the next passenger do with it?

Possible answer
Someone bought it for a friend and then got off at the wrong stop. The next passenger takes a photo of it and puts it back, because returning it feels like admitting they noticed.

A shop has one bright light on and every other light off. What is the owner doing at that moment?

Go further: Who is waiting outside, and why?

Possible answer
The owner is counting coins at the till after a long day. A delivery driver waits at the door with a box the shop never ordered.

A child is drawing a huge, cheerful sun on a grey wall. What made them start?

Go further: What do the adults nearby say when they see it?

Possible answer
They saw the grey wall first and felt bored, so they gave it a sun. One adult stops and asks if they can add a few birds.

Speaking Practice

Stories With a Turn

A turn is the moment the story changes direction. Give your story one.

Tell me about a time you got completely lost. Start with where you thought you were going.

Go further: What did you do in the first minute after you realised?

Possible answer
I followed the wrong exit at the station and ended up in a street I did not know. I stood still, opened the map, and pretended I had meant to explore.

Describe a gift you gave someone that was not expensive but took effort.

Go further: What did their face do when they opened it?

Possible answer
I made my friend a paper book of our class jokes. She read one page and laughed before she said thank you, which was the best part.

Think of a day when the weather ruined a plan. What did you do instead?

Go further: Would you choose that new plan again?

Possible answer
Rain stopped our picnic, so we sat in the station café and drew each other. I would choose it again because we talked more than we would have outside.
Picture Story

Sixty Seconds

Pick three pictures in your mind: a person, a place, and one small object. Link them in one minute.

Your story begins with a door that is open when it should be closed. Who opens it?

Go further: What is the first thing the person sees beyond it?

Possible answer
The cleaning lady opens it because she heard a noise. Beyond it she sees the class hamster sitting on the windowsill, very calm.

The object in your story is an old train ticket. Why does the person keep it?

Go further: What would happen if the ticket were lost?

Possible answer
It came from a trip with his grandmother, so he keeps it in his pencil case. If he lost it, he would still remember the trip but he would feel careless.

Challenge

Describe It Without Naming It

Choose an object in the room. Describe it for ninety seconds without saying its name. Your partner guesses.

Which object will you describe, and which adjective will you hide until the end?

Go further: What clue would make the guess too easy?

Possible answer
I will describe my school bag. I will save the word shabby for last, because the colour gives it away.

When your partner describes something vague, which question do you ask to get a vivid detail?

Go further: Why does that question work better than asking what colour it is?

Possible answer
I ask where they would find it at home. Colour is hard for some people, but a place gives you a picture.
Go Deeper

The Detail Behind the Story

Move away from what happened. Talk about why you remembered it.

Which small detail from your own life would you put in a story for someone who has never met you?

Go further: What would they understand about you from that detail?

Possible answer
The scratched corner of my desk. I have had it since first year and I still sit there, so they would know I like things that stay.

Why do you think people remember smells better than dates?

Go further: Can you give a smell that takes you straight back to one afternoon?

Possible answer
Dates are numbers, but a smell arrives with the whole room. Rain on hot pavement takes me back to a summer walk after school.

If you could show one minute of your life to a pen pal, which minute would you choose?

Go further: What would they be unable to understand without your explanation?

Possible answer
The minute our club practice ends and everyone stops at once. They would not know why we are all laughing, so I would have to explain the joke.
Wrap-Up

Your Story Toolkit

Three sequence words, two vivid adjectives, one small detail. Write them in your notebook and use them this week.

Which word from today will you use tomorrow without thinking about it?

Go further: Where will you put it so you remember?

Possible answer
Glimpse. I will write it on the corner of my timetable.
